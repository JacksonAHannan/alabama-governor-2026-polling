What these estimates mean

These are modeled governor results, not reported votes or district polls. House and Senate counts show districts carried in the governor’s race, not predicted legislative winners.

How to read this model

A material limitation: modeled Jones support among voters aged 18–29 is about 51%, compared with 75–77% in the source poll after undecided allocation. The maps give substantial weight to historical local voting patterns.

Three unique polls set the statewide average. Census demographics, 2024 presidential results, Jones’s local 2020 crossover support, and a limited adjustment for May primary voting distribute that support geographically. The statewide result is matched exactly; demographic and regional polling are softer constraints.

Switch between allocating undecideds in proportion to existing candidate support and splitting them equally. Both scenarios exclude other-candidate and explicitly nonvoting responses. The geographic weights follow 2024 turnout; displayed vote weights are not a forecast of how many people will vote in 2026.

Sources and model assumptions

The statewide average weights the Cygnal, Lowry, and Napolitan surveys by sample size. Numeric demographic and regional polling targets come from Cygnal. Education polarization is estimated from historical voting evidence rather than a 2026 gubernatorial education crosstab.

The model treats race, age, and education population estimates as proxies for the electorate. In legislative drilldown, precinct shapes are clipped to the district and each candidate’s modeled vote weight is scaled by the share of the precinct’s 2020 block voting-age population inside it. Candidate percentages assume uniform preferences within a precinct. Local estimates are not independent observations, and map colors do not show uncertainty.
